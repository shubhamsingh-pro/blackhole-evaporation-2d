What the paper proposes (and what this does and doesn't show)
  1. Capacity. A region holds at most one bit per Planck area of its boundary, S = A/4G (eq. 1.3). The screen above loses pixels as the horizon shrinks.
  2. Spreading. Whatever falls in spreads over the whole stretched horizon at the speed of causality, in about 4MG ln(M²G²) (eqs. 4.10, 4.15), far less than the evaporation time. The orange arc is that growing region. Afterwards a small patch looks thermal and tells you nothing (§6, citing Page): try the arc slider.
  3. Unitarity. Seen from outside, forming and evaporating the hole is an ordinary unitary process (§7). By Page's counting the radiation entropy must turn over at the Page time instead of rising forever, and from then on the radiation carries the message out (the cyan quanta in the main view).
  4. Limits. The paper is a proposal: it concedes (§7) that this outside description cannot say what a freely falling observer sees. Nothing here derives unitarity; the simulation keeps the books assuming it, using Page's counting and the paper's spreading law.
